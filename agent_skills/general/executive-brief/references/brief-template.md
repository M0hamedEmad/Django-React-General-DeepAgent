# Executive brief template

Use only the sections that help the decision.

## Title

Name the subject and reporting period. Avoid vague titles such as “Business Update”.

## Bottom line

In two or three sentences, state what changed, why it matters, and what leadership should decide or watch.

## Material findings

For each finding, include:

- the evidence;
- the business implication;
- the source or confidence level when relevant.

Order findings by consequence, not by the order in which the source material presented them.

## Decision or recommendation

State the recommended choice and rationale. If there are real alternatives, compare them using the criteria that matter to the decision rather than generic pros and cons.

## Risks and unknowns

| Risk or unknown | Likely impact | Evidence/confidence | Mitigation or validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

Do not assign a numerical probability unless a source provides it or the user explicitly asks for a modeled estimate.

## Actions

| Action | Owner | Timing | Status/dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | Unassigned | Not set | ... |

Use “Unassigned” and “Not set” when those details are missing.

## Sources and scope

List authoritative sources, the period covered, material exclusions, and freshness limitations. Keep this compact unless the user requests an audit trail.
